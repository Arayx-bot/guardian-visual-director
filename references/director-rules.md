# Director Rules

## 0. Soul-first Western OC direction

**Design the soul first. Design the character second. Add the cat last.**

Use the complete sequence in [western-oc-character-system.md](western-oc-character-system.md). For the default United States / Western audience, establish Identity, Lore, Personality, Core Contradiction, Archetype/Role, Worldbuilding, Motifs, and Silhouette before facial decoration or cat residue. The character must survive the removal of ears and tail as a complete fantasy/RPG OC.

## 1. Evidence-to-meaning chain

For each conclusion, retain `evidence_ids`, confidence, and the transformation note.

```text
Observed event
→ repeated or contrastive behavioral pattern
→ personality / relationship signal
→ spirit meaning
→ core contradiction and emotional meaning
→ original Guardian identity, archetype, lore, and world role
→ silhouette, body language, costume, prop, and motif logic
→ selective cat residue
→ Mythic Resonance (deep structure only)
```

Do not skip directly from “sits at the door” to “gate deity.” A defensible chain might be: repeated threshold occupancy → boundary-aware low-interruption presence → connection through controlled distance → keeping transitions legible → a contemporary “keeper of chosen crossings” → split silhouette, hinge-like artifact, weight held between two directions.

## 2. Distinction before style

Build a fingerprint across these open axes:

- approach: rush, orbit, wait, test, retreat, return, invite;
- recovery: freeze, inspect, hide, re-emerge, redirect, seek contact;
- space: threshold, high overlook, perimeter, center, enclosure, moving route;
- bond: touch, parallel presence, following, periodic check-in, door waiting, selective return;
- time: dawn, night, arrival, departure, after quiet, irregular;
- strategy: force, alternate path, patient watch, social cue, playful trial;
- contradiction: approach/avoid, independent/attached, still/ready, guarded/curious.

These are examples, not an allowed vocabulary. When evidence shows another axis or expression, name it precisely and add it to the trace.

Before accepting a concept, complete the sentence: “Unlike another cat with the same dominant Spirit, this Guardian expresses it by ___.” That expression must affect at least three of silhouette, prop logic, acting, story beat, camera, or environment.

## 3. Identity translator and cat residue

- Use photo-derived facts only when supplied with confidence. Never invent eye color, markings, or scars.
- Retain all supplied photo traits in the evidence/Canon record, but emphasize only one to three visual residue channels in a single design. Translate unselected traits into palette, rhythm, movement, or private symbolism rather than stacking every feline sign on the body.
- Do not default to ears or tail. Face/eyes, movement, surface markings, subtle hands/feet, ears, or tail are optional channels selected by identity value and evidence. Hands and feet remain functionally humanoid.
- Default target is 0.88 humanoid. Below 0.80 fails unless explicitly authorized; 0.95 is the usual ceiling before feline identity becomes too weak.
- Behavioral feline residue—how the subject watches, retreats, returns, approaches, balances, rests, or preserves distance—has higher priority than decorative anatomy.
- Avoid muzzle-heavy animal heads unless required by a locked canon. Prefer a stylized humanoid-feline facial structure with human-readable brows, cheeks, jaw, and mouth.

## 4. Character design

Before shape/costume design, state:

- the one-sentence Guardian Identity;
- the evidence-derived Personality Pattern;
- the Core Contradiction as two simultaneous truths;
- the Archetype as narrative function;
- minimum viable Lore and Worldbuilding;
- one to three behavior-derived Motifs;
- two or three owner-recognizable resonance anchors.

Archetypes are open narrative functions, never named myth characters or automatic behavior labels. A watchful cat does not automatically become a Hunter. Combine Archetype + Personality + Contradiction into an original role.

Resolve `human_identity_direction` before shape/costume design. Human facial appearance and skin/hair direction belong to identity, not to costume styling. Culture-specific garments, body markings, jewelry, or sacred motifs still require their own evidence and review; an ethnicity reference does not authorize them.

Translate meaning through relationships among forms:

- stable protection: broad base, enclosed negative space, grounded layers;
- observation: lifted focus, narrow directional accents, open sight lines;
- boundary: split planes, seam/hinge motifs, controlled asymmetry;
- return: repeated arcs, reversible closures, inward-turning line;
- exploration: branching construction, modular layers, diagonal openings;
- attachment: paired elements, tether without restraint, echo shapes;
- contradiction: one dominant mass plus a counter-direction, not a random mixed outfit.

Use these as grammar, not style presets. Silhouette must be recognizable in flat black and should communicate one verb.

Costume must have a functional system: base layer, movement layer, identity accent, and one focal detail. Artifact/Symbol should externalize the Guardian’s method, not label its myth. Prefer an original tool, device, clasp, lens, ribbon, compass, key-frame, thread, or light mechanism over sacred objects. Every major element must pass “Do not decorate. Explain.” Remove details that express neither identity, lore, protected subject, past event, behavior, nor relationship.

## 4.1 Rendering style

The project default is `western_fantasy_oc_2d`:

- contemporary Western fantasy/RPG/OC character design with a complete role, lore, readable silhouette, and practical visual storytelling;
- illustrative 2D rendering, confident shape masses, restrained materials, and facial proportions that avoid a generic anime template;
- design clarity compatible with D&D/TTRPG and fantasy OC communities, without copying any franchise;
- East Asian game-art precision may inform finish and costume control, but not the underlying cat-ear character formula.

`2d_anime_oc` remains available only when explicitly selected. In that mode use:

- clean drawn line art with varied line weight;
- simplified graphic facial planes and clearly illustrated anime eyes/brows/mouth/jaw;
- flat local colors and two- or three-step cel shading;
- designed hair locks and fur tufts rather than simulated strands/fibers;
- drawn costume folds rather than physically based fabric;
- 2D painted backgrounds with depth from value/color separation, not photographic depth of field;
- selective highlights only where they help the eyes, identity marks, silhouette, hands, or Artifact.

Negative defaults: photoreal skin, 3D CGI, PBR materials, Unreal/Octane look, subsurface scattering, realistic pores, glossy doll face, volumetric cinematic haze, photographic bokeh, and game-engine screenshot aesthetics. These negatives may be relaxed only when `direction_request.render_style` explicitly selects a non-2D mode.

## 5. Acting and facial performance

Every pose needs intention, readable silhouette, line of action, weight distribution, and asymmetry.

- Eyes/gaze: name target, focus distance, eyelid tension, and whether the gaze leads or follows the body.
- Mouth/jaw: specify compression, parting, breath, or release; avoid a generic smile.
- Head/shoulders/torso/hips/hands/tail should not all repeat the same direction.
- Hands must perform a story action, not merely display the outfit.
- Weight indicates decision: planted = hold; forward = commit; back = assess; split = threshold/contradiction; turning = return or redirection.
- Tail is a secondary acting line, never a decorative S-curve that contradicts emotion.

## 6. Story beat

Always stage a moment with temporal pressure:

- `before`: stable condition immediately before the shot;
- `trigger`: observable change;
- `now`: the exact action caught by the image;
- `emotional_shift`: what becomes newly possible or newly difficult;
- `implied_next`: the action the viewer predicts.

Prefer one decisive beat over a montage. The image should make “what just happened?” and “what will happen next?” answerable.

## 7. Cinematography

Camera choices serve the meaning:

- use eye level for relational equality, low angle for earned resolve, high angle for exposure or spatial reading;
- use 35–50 mm equivalent for environment/action, 70–100 mm for facial acting; avoid extreme lenses unless the beat needs distortion;
- for portrait full or medium-full shots, default the character to 79–82% of canvas height with about 6–9% headroom; keep clear air around ears, coat edges, hands, artifact, and tail;
- place negative space where the gaze or future action points;
- keep foreground triggers subordinate (normally no more than about 12% of the frame) and avoid cropping the locked silhouette merely to increase scale;
- use depth layers to separate subject, trigger, and destination;
- do not use worshipful frontal symmetry, halo staging, or temple iconography as shorthand for “guardian.”

Lighting must preserve identity markings and eye direction. Environment must provide affordances for the beat and stay lower contrast than the face, hands, and artifact.

### Accepted default: game character-select stage

Use `game_character_select_stage` unless the user explicitly requests another presentation:

- one shallow circular raised platform with a clean rim, readable contact shadow, and no elaborate floor pattern;
- two or three broad vertical or slightly offset value fields adapted from the OC palette;
- two large incomplete, offset structural arcs that frame the silhouette without closing into a halo, sacred gateway, or centered magic circle;
- one subdued atmospheric layer, such as simplified clouds, distant rock/city silhouettes, or low-frequency mist, kept below the character in detail and contrast;
- one thin Artifact response line terminating at one small node on an arc; UI-like ticks must be sparse, unlabeled, and subordinate;
- palette and material treatment adapt to the individual Guardian rather than copying another character’s white/gold/blue stage;
- the stage is a presentation system, not a literal location. Exclude furniture, plants, wall art, foreground people, readable interface panels, buttons, logos, and deep room perspective.

The entire platform, arc, field, and response-line system counts as one coherent architectural motif for the complexity budget.

### Minimal background and premium restraint

The project default is `minimal`, not a fully dressed location. Preserve the narrative function of a place while compressing its visual vocabulary:

- one primary architectural motif, such as a doorway, platform, window plane, shoreline, or corridor edge;
- two or three large value/color planes, at most three material families, and zero decorative props by default;
- use overlap, scale, soft value separation, and warm/cool contrast for depth instead of furniture, plants, paintings, ornaments, repeated frames, or surface pattern;
- keep background edge density and local contrast visibly below the character; as a working target, background detail should read at roughly one third or less of the Guardian’s detail density;
- reserve the sharpest edges, smallest details, brightest accent, darkest controlled accents, and highest saturation for face, eyes, hands, Artifact, and identity silhouette;
- convert a story location into an implication, not an inventory. One functional cue is stronger than five descriptive props;
- a prop may exceed the budget only when it is the visible trigger or destination of the Story Beat. Remove all props that do not change the action.

“Premium” means disciplined hierarchy and confident empty area, not expensive décor, glossy materials, marble pattern, excessive reflections, cinematic haze, or a luxury-interior look.

### Character value architecture and palette relationship

Perceived finish depends more on organized value masses than on whether the costume is light or dark:

- establish a dominant value family, a supporting middle-value family, and a controlled darkest anchor;
- place dark or mid-value structure where it clarifies anatomy and costume construction: inner layer, collar/shoulder frame, waist, harness, leg rhythm, boots, and Artifact mechanics;
- reserve a clean value window around the face, eyes, acting hand, and Artifact. Hair, ears, and background must not collapse into one pale or dark mass;
- distribute detail by hierarchy: face and identity marks first; silhouette-defining costume breaks second; repeated buckles, straps, seams, and texture only where they reinforce form;
- echo one or two character colors in the stage at lower saturation, but create local light/dark or warm/cool separation along the entire identity silhouette;
- do not solve a pale character by arbitrarily recoloring identity fur. Strengthen costume underlayers, edge fields, and background value placement instead;
- test at thumbnail size and in grayscale. The head, torso direction, hands, tail, and major costume architecture must remain legible.

### Visual-weight balance and focal hierarchy

Balance means asymmetrical equilibrium, not equal objects on both sides. Evaluate the composition as a small grayscale thumbnail as well as in color:

- one primary focal point: Guardian face/eyes first, acting hand or Artifact second, story environment third;
- background people and human-shaped silhouettes are off by default; if a Story Beat truly requires one, keep it small, faceless, low-contrast, outside the Guardian silhouette, and below the protagonist in detail and value contrast;
- do not leave one large near-black blob isolated in a pale peripheral area; distribute supporting dark anchors through architecture, costume rhythm, floor reflection, or frame edges without creating a second focal point;
- keep the perceived center of visual gravity on or immediately around the Guardian, even when the layout is asymmetric;
- avoid silhouette tangencies: no background head, torso, vertical edge, or high-contrast object should appear to grow from or split the Guardian’s head, ears, hands, tail, or coat;
- remove any optional element that competes with the face, breaks the warm/cool value rhythm, or makes the image feel as if it will tip toward one corner.

## 8. Prompt compilation

Compile from structured fields, never the reverse. Build explicit fields for Guardian Identity, Core Personality, Core Contradiction, Archetype, Lore Context, Silhouette, Body Language, Costume Logic, Symbolic Motifs, Props, Feline Residue, Facial Direction, Material Language, and Visual Tone. Then map them into the stable layered prompt contract. Put the soul/identity first and selected cat residue late. Use concise declarative clauses and keep layer labels for APIs that tolerate them. If an API expects plain text, join layers with paragraph breaks. Put P0 identity constraints in both Identity and Constraints layers. Negative prompt items should name visible failures, not abstract judgments.

## 9. Distinctiveness and personal resonance

Before generation, identify two or three features that could only come from this cat's evidence. Check identity, lore, contradiction, silhouette, motif, and owner-specific distinctiveness. If another cat could inherit the brief unchanged, redesign the character layer rather than adding more cat anatomy.

Reject repeated defaults such as white hair, cloak, glowing eyes, magical weapon, cat ears, and moon symbol. When recent designs converge, rotate body type, age impression, posture, costume construction, material family, silhouette, prop family, or motif family.

Score behavior fidelity, personality specificity, contradiction strength, identity originality, lore coherence, symbolic coherence, silhouette distinctiveness, visual storytelling, feline subtlety, and personal resonance from 0–10. Score genericness risk from 0–10 where lower is better. Trigger one design-layer retry when Personal Resonance or Identity Originality is below 7, or Genericness Risk exceeds 4.
