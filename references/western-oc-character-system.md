# Western OC Character System

## Governing principle

**Design the soul first. Design the character second. Add the cat last.**

The intended first read is a complete humanoid fantasy being with an identity, history, role, silhouette, and visual logic. The feline source should become unmistakable only on the second read. Never begin with ears, tail, fur color, hairstyle, or a fashionable human body.

The default audience is the United States / Western OC, fantasy, RPG, D&D, and TTRPG community. Borrow finish, precision, and controlled costume detail from East Asian game art when useful, but keep the underlying design identity-first, lore-driven, personality-driven, and visually readable.

## Required derivation order

1. **Observed Behavior** — record literal events and evidence IDs.
2. **Personality Pattern** — name a stable manner of acting, not a generic adjective.
3. **Core Contradiction** — identify two evidence-supported truths that coexist.
4. **Emotional Meaning** — explain why the pattern matters to the owner.
5. **Guardian Identity** — state who this being is in one role-rich sentence.
6. **Archetype + Role** — choose an open narrative function, not a named myth figure.
7. **Lore** — define why it exists, what it protects, its world, its symbols, and its defining vow/pattern.
8. **Symbolic Motifs** — choose one to three motifs caused by behavior and meaning.
9. **Silhouette + Body Language** — design the black-shape read, stance, weight, prop gap, and negative space.
10. **Costume + Props** — give every major element a narrative or functional reason.
11. **Cat Residue** — apply one to three selective visual channels only after the humanlike character works without them.
12. **Final Guardian** — verify personal resonance, distinctiveness, and prompt constraints.

## Eight core dimensions

### Identity

Answer “Who is this being?” before appearance. Compress personality, emotional meaning, protected subject, and world role into an original title such as “Keeper of the Way Back.” Identity is not a job label.

### Lore

Keep lore concise but causal. Answer:

- why the being exists;
- what it protects;
- what rules its world recognizes;
- why it carries its motifs and props;
- which event, instinct, vow, or repeated pattern defines it.

### Personality

Convert labels into stable patterns. Prefer “boundary-sensitive explorer,” “ritual-dependent companion,” or “independent but attachment-driven follower” over “shy,” “curious,” or “clingy.” Every pattern cites evidence IDs.

### Core contradiction

Prioritize “two truths at once.” Examples: afraid yet compelled to return; autonomous in space yet dependent in relationship; cautious in movement yet bold in attachment. A single positive adjective is insufficient.

### Archetype / role

Archetype is a narrative function, not a personality conclusion or myth match. The library is open: Keeper, Sentinel, Witness, Wanderer, Seeker, Guide, Messenger, Protector, Companion, Healer, Trickster, Threshold Guardian, Pathkeeper, Watcher, Gatekeeper, Archivist, Beacon, Wayfinder, and new evidence-derived roles are valid.

### Silhouette

Design body proportion, stance, shoulder width, weight distribution, coat/armor mass, head shape, prop contour, and negative space before facial decoration. The flat-black silhouette should communicate one verb and remain distinguishable from other Guardians.

### Visual storytelling

**Do not decorate. Explain.** Every major costume, prop, material, or motif must express identity, personality, lore, protected subject, past event, behavior, or owner relationship. Remove anything that cannot answer why it exists.

### Personal resonance

The commercial goal is not merely “cool character” but “that is my cat.” Require two or three owner-recognizable behavioral or private signals. If the concept still works unchanged for another cat, regenerate the character-design layer.

## Supporting dimensions

### Worldbuilding

Describe the world rule that makes the Guardian necessary, its function in that world, and how other beings understand it. A backdrop description alone is not worldbuilding.

### Motif

Choose one to three behavior-derived motifs. Knot, lantern, threshold, window, thread, bell, key, orbit, feather, branch, doorway, tide, constellation, and similar families are examples, not a fixed list. Avoid direct sacred-symbol copies.

### Distinctiveness

Check identity, lore, contradiction, silhouette, motif, and owner-specific distinctiveness. When recent Guardians repeat white hair, cloak, glowing eyes, magical weapon, cat ears, or moon symbols, change body type, age impression, posture, costume construction, material, silhouette, prop family, or motif family.

## Cat residue

Cat residue is subtle, selective, and identity-preserving. Use one to three visual channels among face, movement, hands/feet, and surface. Do not default to ears + tail + fur + whiskers + claws.

Behavioral feline residue has the highest priority: how the subject watches, retreats, returns, approaches, rests, balances, or maintains distance. Decorative feline residue is subordinate.

Default proportions:

- human visual structure: 80–95%;
- feline residue: 5–20%.

The first read is “humanoid fantasy being.” The second read is “something unmistakably feline.” Full animal heads, furry bodies, mascot anatomy, or quadruped bodies require explicit user direction.

## Mythic resonance

Keep the existing myth layer but treat it as resonance or lineage after the original Guardian exists. Myth may contribute archetypal resonance, symbolic lineage, narrative pattern, cultural context, or motif inspiration. It must never determine a copied deity body, face, costume, regalia, sacred object, weapon, or pose.

Correct order: observed behavior → emotional meaning → original Guardian → mythic resonance.

## Final gates

Ask both questions:

1. If ears and tail were removed, would this remain a complete and unique character?
2. Without explanation, could the owner recognize something emotionally true about their cat?

If the first answer is no, return to Identity/Lore/Silhouette. If the second is no, return to Behavior Evidence and Personal Resonance.

Internal scoring uses 0–10 for behavior fidelity, personality specificity, contradiction strength, identity originality, lore coherence, symbolic coherence, silhouette distinctiveness, visual storytelling, feline subtlety, and personal resonance. Genericness risk also uses 0–10, where lower is better. Regenerate the character-design layer once when personal resonance or identity originality is below 7, or genericness risk is above 4.
